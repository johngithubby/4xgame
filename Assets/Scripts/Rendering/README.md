# Rendering Scripts

## Files

- `PrototypeCharacterFactory.cs`: Builds collider-free survivor and zombie character hierarchies, instantiates the optimized licensed 3D SWAT model on all three visible survivors and every visible zombie, shares explicit diffuse/normal/specular PBR materials, assigns deterministic survivor wardrobe presets and zombie appearance seeds, hides fallback survivor cards and zombie weapons, attaches infected face/wound details, bakes each survivor's imported weapon skins into rigid aim-pivot renderers, assigns Mixamo-retargeted controllers, and replaces every hidden prototype muzzle with an anchor centred on the rendered barrel opening.
- `PrototypeGeometryFactory.cs`: Creates collider-free placeholder cube meshes, rounded sphere meshes, cylinder meshes, regular prism meshes, flat road planes, and upright texture-card planes for runtime-generated scenes, avoiding Unity's primitive collider path in iOS simulator builds.
- `PrototypeHumanoidAnimationStyle.cs`: Names the procedural animation profiles used by generated survivor and zombie rigs.
- `PrototypeHumanoidAnimator.cs`: Drives generated fallback-survivor and zombie joint chains plus their procedural aiming/recoil; it bypasses all three complete imported SWAT hierarchies, including their parent poses, so old sine-driven motion cannot layer over Mixamo clips.
- `PrototypeMaterialFactory.cs`: Creates tinted opaque depth-writing placeholder materials for runtime-generated prototype geometry, alpha-blended textured materials for reference cutout cards, always-visible transparent text materials, and always-visible solid tracer materials for world-space combat feedback by probing a short list of common Unity shaders.
- `ProceduralSoldierRearTexture.cs`: Generates the rear-facing woman soldier cutout used by chase-camera survivor views from the approved readable front cutout's alpha silhouette and paint detail, then layers rear armor, helmet, ponytail, backpack, a single compact raised shoulder rifle with supporting forearms, teal suit panels, gray plates, and cyan lights without reusing the front-facing PNG backward.
- `ReferenceModelFacingVisibility.cs`: Shows the reference-derived rear soldier decal in the minigame chase camera and switches to the approved front soldier decal only when a camera crosses to the zombie-facing side of the survivor root.
- `SwatSurvivorLocomotionAnimator.cs`: Rehydrates its runtime-only Animator and squad references when a scene-built character awakens, enforces full base-layer influence and continuous evaluation, receives authoritative movement changes directly from `PlayerSquad`, switches the imported controller's `Moving` bool without applying root motion, and starts commander/scout/heavy survivors at stable one-third-cycle offsets so the squad does not run in lockstep. It applies symmetrical post-retarget hip/boot constraints and rotates the imported `b_Body` weapon pivot from its live stock-to-muzzle joints so the rigidly rendered rifle follows the current zombie point before and throughout each shot. A tracer-lifetime hold keeps that pose fully aligned until the orange trace is gone, after which the rifle may recover to its locomotion pose. State/root-displacement fallback remains for isolated factory tests.
- `SwatSurvivorAppearance.cs`: Defines commander, scout, and heavy wardrobe presets. It reconstructs scene-loaded `MaterialPropertyBlock` colours from one serialized preset, replaces the nearly black suit albedo with distinct dark navy, charcoal/burgundy, or forest/olive colours, and toggles helmet, balaclava, belt, pads, vest, and armor layers to create three different silhouettes while sharing the same meshes and PBR materials.
- `SwatZombieAppearance.cs`: Uses a stable local random stream to choose three high-saturation garment colours, five large UV-space tear ellipses, ragged-edge variation, and a different surviving tactical-gear mask for every authored spawn. The seed carries spawn order in its low nibble so the first nine consecutive zombies receive different dominant palette entries instead of producing near-duplicate colours by chance. It sends the values through `MaterialPropertyBlock`, so all zombies still share one suit material and mesh while alpha-tested holes reveal the already-skinned infected body below.
- `SwatZombieAnimator.cs`: Reuses the tracked Mixamo rifle-walk foot exchange at a slower speed, then adds a torso-driven hunch, deterministic lateral drunken sway, sharp near-fall/catch pulses, unequal knee collapse and a dragging step, independent full-chain arm reaching that replaces the source rifle hold, hand agitation, head/jaw motion, pulsing bloody eyes, and swinging drool. Anatomical-side and permissive toe-out constraints prevent leg crossing or sideways feet, while per-frame support-foot grounding keeps the lower boot on the road. All visual motion stays below the stationary gameplay target root.

## Compile

Unity compiles this directory through the main `LaneSurvivor.Runtime` assembly.

## Behavior

Runtime bootstrap code uses these factories for track surfaces, buildings, gates, the three-person imported Humanoid squad, imported Humanoid zombies, hidden fallback rigs, muzzle effects, feedback labels, and remaining prototype objects. The commander, scout, heavy, and zombies all reuse one optimized 59,288-triangle FBX and the same 1024-pixel-capped PBR texture channels. Survivor property blocks replace the source suit's nearly black flat RGB with readable dark wardrobe colours while retaining its normal-map folds; equipment visibility produces full tactical, lightweight, and heavy silhouettes. Every survivor retargets the complete Mixamo lowered-rifle idle/run controller, receives the squad's authoritative movement state, owns a real visible rifle and barrel anchor, and participates in round-robin target-aware shooting. Generated survivor meshes and front/rear decals remain serialized fallbacks but stay disabled whenever the imported model is available.

Zombies override the controller with the calmer in-place rifle walk, remove every visible firearm, and layer an asymmetric drunk stumble while gameplay roots remain fixed for targeting. Their vivid upper/lower/accent colours, five deterministic ragged holes, varied missing gear, bloody eyes, wounds, and drool remain per-instance property-block or attached-geometry effects. Standard Humanoid gait constraints keep survivor feet aligned with +Z travel, while target-aware late-frame correction rotates each selected survivor's rigid MPX from its live stock-to-muzzle line. Flash and tracer effects stay attached to that same rendered barrel until their lifetime ends.
