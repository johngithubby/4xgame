# Rendering Scripts

## Files

- `PrototypeCharacterFactory.cs`: Builds collider-free survivor and zombie character hierarchies, instantiates the optimized licensed 3D SWAT model on both the leader and visible zombies, resolves its external diffuse/normal/specular channels into lit PBR materials, shares one immutable infected material palette across zombie instances, hides zombie weapons and face coverings, attaches enlarged bloody eyes, blood trails, wounds, and jaw-following drool, retains the generated zombie as an invisible fallback, bakes only the survivor's imported weapon skins into rigid PBR renderers below their shared aim pivot, assigns Mixamo-retargeted controllers, replaces the leader's hidden prototype muzzle with an anchor centred on the foremost vertices of the rendered muzzle opening whose forward axis follows the imported barrel-base-to-muzzle bore, and retains two reference-decal wing survivors.
- `PrototypeGeometryFactory.cs`: Creates collider-free placeholder cube meshes, rounded sphere meshes, cylinder meshes, regular prism meshes, flat road planes, and upright texture-card planes for runtime-generated scenes, avoiding Unity's primitive collider path in iOS simulator builds.
- `PrototypeHumanoidAnimationStyle.cs`: Names the procedural animation profiles used by generated survivor and zombie rigs.
- `PrototypeHumanoidAnimator.cs`: Drives generated wing-survivor and zombie joint chains plus their procedural aiming/recoil; it bypasses the complete imported SWAT leader hierarchy, including its parent pose, so old sine-driven motion cannot layer over Mixamo clips.
- `PrototypeMaterialFactory.cs`: Creates tinted opaque depth-writing placeholder materials for runtime-generated prototype geometry, alpha-blended textured materials for reference cutout cards, always-visible transparent text materials, and always-visible solid tracer materials for world-space combat feedback by probing a short list of common Unity shaders.
- `ProceduralSoldierRearTexture.cs`: Generates the rear-facing woman soldier cutout used by chase-camera survivor views from the approved readable front cutout's alpha silhouette and paint detail, then layers rear armor, helmet, ponytail, backpack, a single compact raised shoulder rifle with supporting forearms, teal suit panels, gray plates, and cyan lights without reusing the front-facing PNG backward.
- `ReferenceModelFacingVisibility.cs`: Shows the reference-derived rear soldier decal in the minigame chase camera and switches to the approved front soldier decal only when a camera crosses to the zombie-facing side of the survivor root.
- `SwatSurvivorLocomotionAnimator.cs`: Rehydrates its runtime-only Animator and squad references when a scene-built character awakens, enforces full base-layer influence and continuous evaluation, receives authoritative movement changes directly from `PlayerSquad`, switches the imported controller's `Moving` bool without applying root motion, applies symmetrical post-retarget hip/boot constraints, and rotates the imported `b_Body` weapon pivot from its live stock-to-muzzle joints so the rigidly rendered rifle follows the current zombie point before and throughout each shot. A tracer-lifetime hold keeps that pose fully aligned until the orange trace is gone, after which the rifle may recover to its locomotion pose. State/root-displacement fallback remains for isolated factory tests.
- `SwatZombieAnimator.cs`: Reuses the tracked Mixamo rifle-walk foot exchange at a slower speed, then adds a torso-driven hunch, deterministic lateral drunken sway, sharp near-fall/catch pulses, unequal knee collapse and a dragging step, independent full-chain arm reaching that replaces the source rifle hold, hand agitation, head/jaw motion, pulsing bloody eyes, and swinging drool. Anatomical-side and permissive toe-out constraints prevent leg crossing or sideways feet, while per-frame support-foot grounding keeps the lower boot on the road. All visual motion stays below the stationary gameplay target root.

## Compile

Unity compiles this directory through the main `LaneSurvivor.Runtime` assembly.

## Behavior

Runtime bootstrap code uses these factories for flat track surfaces, pentagon base/HQ prisms, gate cards, the 3D SWAT leader beside two reference-decal wing survivors, imported Humanoid zombies, hidden fallback skeleton rigs, muzzle flares, shot tracers, feedback labels, and remaining simple prototype objects. The optimized SWAT leader and zombies share one 59,288-triangle FBX and explicit 1024-pixel-capped PBR texture channels. The leader's Unity Humanoid-compatible bones retarget complete Mixamo lowered-rifle idle and rifle-run poses on one fully weighted base layer, matching the game's 4.2 m/s travel pace. Zombies instead override every controller state with the calmer in-place rifle walk, remove every visible firearm, and layer an asymmetric drunk stumble while gameplay roots remain fixed for targeting. Enlarged red eyes, dark pupils, socket blood, facial streaks, chest/head wounds, and green drool are small collider-free meshes parented to live face, jaw, head, and chest bones. Their generated predecessors remain disabled fallback renderers only, preventing a doubled silhouette. The survivor run keeps every resampled source key and its authored loop rather than Unity curve reduction or loop-pose redistribution flattening the foot-clearance arc. Standard Humanoid twist/stretch distribution plus symmetrical late-frame hip and boot constraints keep each survivor leg on its anatomical side and limit both feet against the objective +Z lane-travel heading instead of trusting a potentially skewed imported rest pose. Controller crossfades, gameplay-owned world movement, continuous Animator evaluation, and complete procedural-parent isolation prevent the old sine motion or idle pose from contaminating the result. The visible survivor rifle is a separate imported hierarchy below the right hand: its weapon-only skinned renderers are baked once into rigid pivot-local meshes because the source skin does not honor helper-bone aim reliably, then a target-aware late-frame correction measures the live `s_Stock`-to-muzzle line and rotates `b_Body`. The muzzle anchor used by flashes and tracers is positioned at the averaged centre of the rigid muzzle proxy's foremost rendered vertices and parented directly to the same `b_Body` pivot, so VFX begin on the barrel opening without drifting with recessed FBX helper bones. Each shot refreshes a hold covering the tracer's complete lifetime plus a one-frame safety margin, so the aim cannot recover leftward until its orange trace has stopped. The rifle geometry, muzzle anchor, flash, and tracer therefore share one transform and one target line. Built-in Standard materials receive deterministic albedo and normal-map assignments, source specular maps where provided, and restrained smoothness/metallic response, replacing the old colour-only fallbacks. Both legacy leader cards and their camera-facing switcher remain disabled after the FBX loads, while the two wing members retain the previous decals as an immediate in-game quality and performance comparison.
